**1. Purpose.** 

The *Law of the Network State* is a build-in-public research and legal-engineering initiative exploring how law, governance, and institutions can evolve for a networked, AI-native world. The project investigates how legal authority can become interoperable, verifiable, programmable, and accountable while preserving human agency, institutional legitimacy, and the diversity of legal systems. Its central research question is:
*"How can we design legal infrastructure that enables people, organizations, autonomous agents, and emerging institutions to coordinate across jurisdictions without requiring a single global legal system?"*
This repository documents the public research, conceptual frameworks, legal specifications, institutional experiments, and lessons emerging from that inquiry. 

**2. Philosophy.** The project proceeds from several working propositions:

2.1. Law is infrastructure. Legal systems provide the underlying architecture through which rights, authority, obligations, and institutions become operational.

2.2. Jurisdiction is becoming layered. Territorial authority remains essential for human activity, but all emerging technologies are cross-border in nature and require contractual, institutional, technological, and network-based governance.

2.3. A technical audit is not a legal audit. Cryptographic signatures, smart contracts, and AI-generated decisions do not independently establish legal authority or enforceability.

2.4. Interoperability does not require uniformity. Different legal systems can cooperate through shared standards, recognition mechanisms, and procedural interfaces.

2.5. Human agency and accountability must be designed into institutions. Automation should not eliminate meaningful oversight, contestability, or remedies, and should not erode personal agency, privacy, property, human rights and freedoms, etc.

2.6. Institutional innovation must be tested. New governance models should demonstrate measurable value before making claims of legal or political transformation.

**3. Pathways.**

**Phase 1 — Intellectual Foundations**

*Status: In progress*

**Objective:** develop the historical, jurisprudential, and institutional foundations for understanding the evolution of law beyond exclusively territorial models of governance.

**Phase 2 — Salons**

*Status: In progress*

**Objective:** create a structured dialogue among legal scholars, historians, technologists, institutional designers, policymakers, and frontier builders.

The Salons are intended to test assumptions, expose disagreements, and generate research outputs, not simply validate a predetermined network-state thesis.

**Phase 3 — Legal Primitives**

*Status: In development*

**Objective:** translate the research into clear legal concepts that can support future specifications and experiments.

Initial Primitives are Agency, Bounded Sovereignty, Nested Jurisdiction, and B2B Lex Mercatoria.

**Phase 4 — Interoperability and Institutional Architecture**

*Status: Proposed*

**Objective:** investigate how distinct legal institutions can exchange legally meaningful information without surrendering their independence or substantive differences.

**Phase 5 — Models and Sandboxes**

*Status: Proposed*

**Objective:** identify legally credible environments for testing new institutional arrangements.

**Phase 6 — Minimum Viable Jurisdiction**

*Status: Candidate project — not yet launched*

**Objective:** design the smallest legally credible institutional experiment capable of demonstrating measurable improvements in cross-border coordination. For the purposes of this research, a Minimum Viable Jurisdiction (MVJ) is a proposed institutional design concept, not a claim of public sovereignty or a recognized category of international law. An initial pilot may be a private-law institutional arrangement rather than a jurisdiction exercising public authority.

**Candidate Pilot: Cross-Border Commercial Commons** 

The pilot would operate within existing legal frameworks and would not claim regulatory exemptions, public coercive powers, or independent sovereignty.

**Phase 7 — Proof-of-Value**

*Status: Framework development*

**Objective:** evaluate whether proposed legal infrastructure creates meaningful value beyond the automation of existing legal tasks.

**4. Intellectual Provenance**

The Law of the Network State builds upon a substantial body of existing legal, historical, economic, and technical research. Relevant intellectual traditions include legal pluralism, polycentric governance, private international law, institutional economics, commercial arbitration, computational law, and legal engineering. The project will also examine contemporary initiatives and open-source communities.
The project welcomes substantive engagement from researchers, legal professionals, technologists, institutional designers, policymakers, and other interested contributors. 

The ambition of the project is not to replace existing legal systems with a single digital jurisdiction. The thesis is that legal infrastructure can become programmable while preserving the elements that make law legitimate: recognized authority, human agency, institutional accountability, due process, and effective remedies.
This roadmap is a living research document. It records proposed directions and evaluation criteria, not legal advice, regulatory authorization, completed implementations, or guaranteed project outcomes. Ixian will employ agentic systems to facilitate this body of work.
